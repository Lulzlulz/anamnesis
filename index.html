He answers only what you ask him. He will not volunteer anything — real patients don't.

What was actually wrong with him

Anvar has a bleeding gastric ulcer. He has taken diclofenac every day for a year for his knee, bought over the counter, without a doctor. His stool has been black for three weeks. He has lost six kilograms. His brother died of stomach cancer at sixty-five, which is why he is frightened, and why he did not say so.

None of that arrives on its own. Every piece of it requires a specific question, asked out loud, in a language the student does not have.

What Anamnesis is

An AI simulated patient that speaks the clinical language of the country a student is training in, so that a student can practise the one skill they currently cannot practise: taking a complete history, alone, from someone who does not share their language. Every session is scored against the same rubric a human examiner would use, and the student is shown exactly what they failed to ask.

The buyer is the university, not the student. Universities are accredited on clinical competence and carry the cost of graduates who cannot communicate.

What is built, and what is not

  • BuiltThis consultation. A scripted case with a hidden diagnosis and rubric scoring — the interaction model, working.
  • BuiltA speech-recognition benchmark for clinical Uzbek and Russian, at anamnesis-bench.vercel.app. Measuring whether the words survive the microphone.
  • Not builtLive voice. Uzbek speech recognition is the hard engineering problem and we are measuring it before we build on it, not after.
  • Not builtGenerated cases. This one is written by hand.
  • Not runThe validation study comparing our rubric scoring against blinded human examiners. It is designed. Running it is why we are applying.

This demonstration makes no diagnostic claim, involves no real patient, and collects no data. Nothing you type leaves this page. The case is fictional and written for teaching. Uzbek dialogue is pending review by a native clinical speaker.

Anamnesis is built in Bukhara by a medical student who could not take a history from his own patients. Co-author, Fundamental Principles of the Surgical Department (Bukhara, 2026), approved by the Ministry of Health of Uzbekistan.
